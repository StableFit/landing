Being a good coach certainly matters. But a client does not always understand which solution will help with their problem - or why.

Especially when you open a consult with:

- "I'll write you a program"
- "We'll keep nutrition under control"
- "I have a lot of experience"

The person has not said a word about themselves yet, and you are already trying to sell something.

To keep a consult from turning into a monologue about you, it helps to know a few simple psychological techniques.

## 1. Let the client talk

You can be the best specialist in the room. But if the whole consult is you talking about yourself, you never find out what the person actually needs.

Ask:

- What exactly do you want to change?
- Why does this matter right now?
- What have you already tried?
- What got in the way of getting a result?

Understand the person first. Then offer a solution.

## 2. Help them name the real request

Someone can walk in saying:

- "I need a training program"

And mean something completely different.

For example, they do not know how to train on their own, they are afraid of doing it wrong, or they keep dropping training after two weeks.

Your job is to ask a few clarifying questions and understand which problem they actually want to solve.

Then check:

- "Am I right that you don't just need a program - you need a system that helps you train regularly and not quit in a month?"

After that, it becomes clearer to them what they are actually looking for.

## 3. Light up the problem they came to solve

Help the person see what happens if nothing changes. And what can go wrong if they try to fix it on their own again.

For example:

- "If you keep going like this - what do you think will be different in six months?"
- "You've already tried training on your own a few times. What do you think might get in the way and make you drop it again?"

The point is not to scare the client. It is to light up the same problem they brought to you.

Then, before you offer a solution, the person sees more clearly why something has to change at all.

## 4. Find out how the client sees their solution

Ask directly:

- "What does the ideal way of working with a coach look like for you?"

Maybe training at home matters to them.

Or they want to stay in touch all the time.

Or the opposite - they do not want to send reports every day and get messages at 7 a.m.

When you understand the format they are looking for, you can honestly say whether your way of working fits.

And you do not have to sell everyone the same thing.

## 5. Show that you have already worked with situations like this

A person may think:

- "My case is special"
- "Nothing will help me anymore"

If you have similar experience - tell them about it.

No made-up "100% results" and magic promises. Just:

- "I already had a client in a similar situation. We started small - and gradually built regular training"

A concrete example often builds more trust than ten minutes talking about your experience.

The main thing - do not try to sell training to every person who just showed up for a consult.

First understand their request.

Then figure out whether you can actually help.

And only after that offer your way of working.

Because good sales are not when you talk about yourself beautifully.

They are when the person leaves the conversation thinking: "Yes. This is exactly what I need."
