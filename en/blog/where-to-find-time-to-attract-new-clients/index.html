Where to find time to attract new clients?

Blog · 21 September 2026

If the first question you ask yourself is "where do I get new clients?", after you learn every acquisition method the next one shows up:

"And where, exactly, do I get the time for that?"

Reels, Threads, Instagram, personal brand, content plan - and you still have to grow yourself somehow. Below is what you can actually do without inventing a new job called "coach + SMM + accountant".

1. Start with your own time management

Look at where your time actually goes. Social scrolling, games, series, endless "just 5 more minutes" - cut at least some of that.

Even 8-10 free hours a week can already become time to find clients. That does not mean working 24/7.

Just replace some cheap dopamine: a series with a film, social feeds with a book. Rest you actually recover from.

2. Look at how you work now

Where does the work time actually go?

Do you spend too long writing programs? Hunt for client data across chats? Reply the moment a client messages? Film a video for every exercise? Keep measurements in Excel? Check nutrition in a separate app?

Or you just procrastinate, because there is already so much work you do not even want to start?

StableFit takes a chunk of that routine. But the other point matters more: without a system, any new task just adds more mess. A new reel will not help if the client card is still split between a chat, a spreadsheet, and the camera roll.

3. Make finding clients part of the job

Do not wait until three hours free up and inspiration appears to "finally do content".

Block time in the calendar and work on acquisition regularly. You do not need to shoot 15 reels in a day when a client leaves. Better to do two a week, steadily.

In training and nutrition, results come from consistency. Your marketing is the same.

4. If you still have no time - look deeper

If you have many clients, no time, still not enough money, and the first three points did not work - maybe you do not need even more clients.

Maybe you need to look at your prices.

Yes, some people may leave. But if one client takes a lot of your time and brings in too little money, endlessly adding new ones is not the best strategy.

This is not advice to raise your rate every two months. But checking whether your prices match your load is worth it.

5. And the main thing - do not overcomplicate it

You do not need 15 new tools to start.

You need to see where your time goes now, what of that actually matters, and what you can drop or automate.
